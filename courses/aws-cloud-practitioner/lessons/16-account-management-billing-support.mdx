      Billing and pricing is roughly a quarter of the CLF-C02 exam, so the
      tools in this lesson are heavily tested. The theme is simple: AWS gives
      you services to **estimate** costs (Pricing Calculator, TCO
      Calculator), **track** them (Billing Dashboard, Cost Allocation Tags,
      Cost & Usage Report, Cost Explorer), **monitor** them (Billing Alarms,
      AWS Budgets, Cost Anomaly Detection), and **reduce** them (Savings
      Plans, Compute Optimizer) — and then a set of **support plans** to help
      when things go wrong.

      ## AWS Organizations: manage many accounts

      **AWS Organizations** is a **global service** that manages **multiple
      AWS accounts** from one place. The main account is called the
      **management account** (formerly the master account) and the others are
      **member accounts** (formerly child accounts). Accounts are grouped
      into **Organizational Units (OUs)** — by business unit (Sales, Retail,
      Finance), by environment (Prod, Dev, Test), or by project.

      Organizations gives you three exam-critical benefits:

      - **Consolidated billing**: all accounts are paid by the management
        account, producing **one bill** — member accounts do not need their
        own payment method
      - **Aggregated usage**: usage from all accounts is combined, so you
        reach **volume discount** tiers (e.g., S3 pricing tiers) faster and
        get bigger discounts
      - **Reserved Instance / Savings Plan sharing**: a discount purchased in
        one account can be used by instances in other accounts, maximizing
        savings. Sharing can be turned off for any account, including the
        management account

      You can also **automate account creation** through the Organizations
      API (useful for spinning up sandbox accounts programmatically), and
      restrict what accounts can do with **Service Control Policies (SCPs)**.

      :::tip **Exam tip:**
      Consolidated billing is a *cost* feature, not a security feature. A
      question that mentions "one bill for all accounts," "volume discounts
      across accounts," or "sharing Reserved Instances between accounts" is
      asking for **AWS Organizations**. A question that mentions "limit what
      a member account can do" is asking for **SCPs**.
      :::

      ## Service Control Policies (SCPs) & Control Tower

      An **SCP** is a policy that **whitelists or blacklists IAM actions** at
      the **OU or account level**. It is the exam's favorite Organizations
      topic, so get the rules exactly right:

      - SCPs apply to **all users and roles** in a member account —
        **including the account's root user**. If an SCP denies EC2, even an
        admin with full IAM permissions cannot use EC2
      - SCPs **do not apply to the management account** — they have no effect
        there
      - SCPs **do not affect service-linked roles** (the service roles that
        other AWS services use to integrate with Organizations)
      - An SCP must contain an **explicit Allow** — by default nothing is
        allowed at the boundary (IAM policies inside the account remain
        allow-by-default)

      Typical use cases: restrict certain services in production accounts
      (e.g., no EMR), or enforce compliance by disabling services that are
      not PCI compliant yet.

      **AWS Control Tower** sits **on top of Organizations** and gives you an
      easy way to set up and govern a secure, compliant multi-account
      environment with a few clicks. It automates account setup and ongoing
      policy management through **guardrails** (implemented as SCPs) and
      provides a compliance dashboard.

      :::tip **Exam tip:**
      The classic trap: "the management account is also restricted by SCPs."
      Wrong — SCPs apply to **OUs and member accounts**, never to the
      management account. Second trap: "SCPs replace IAM." No — SCPs are a
      boundary *between* accounts; IAM still controls permissions *inside*
      each account.
      :::

      ## AWS pricing fundamentals

      AWS pricing follows **four pricing models**:

      | Model | Idea | Exam keyword |
      |-------|------|--------------|
      | **Pay as you go** | Pay only for what you use; start, stop, and delete anytime | "agile", "scale to demand" |
      | **Save when you reserve** | Commit 1 or 3 years for a predictable budget | "minimize risk", "long-term requirements" |
      | **Pay less by using more** | Volume-based discounts (e.g., S3 tiers); AWS passes on economies of scale | "volume discounts", "as AWS grows" |
      | **Free services & Free Tier** | Some services are free (IAM, VPC, consolidated billing); others have free tiers | "IAM is free", "12 months free" |

      Some services are free themselves but bill for what they create —
      **Elastic Beanstalk, CloudFormation, and Auto Scaling groups** are free,
      but the EC2 instances, load balancers, and other resources they
      provision are not.

      ### EC2 pricing recap

      | Model | Discount vs On-Demand | Commitment |
      |-------|----------------------|------------|
      | **On-Demand** | None — per-second billing (Linux/Windows) with a 60-second minimum | None |
      | **Reserved Instances** | Up to **75%** | 1 or 3 years; all / partial / no upfront (more upfront = bigger discount) |
      | **Spot Instances** | Up to **90%** | Bid for unused capacity — instances can be reclaimed |
      | **Savings Plans** | EC2 Savings Plan up to **72%**; Compute Savings Plan up to **66%** | Commit to a dollar-per-hour amount for 1 or 3 years |
      | **Dedicated Hosts** | On-Demand or Reserved pricing | Physical server dedicated to you |

      Compute Savings Plans are the most flexible: they apply to **EC2
      instances, Fargate containers, and Lambda** regardless of family,
      region, size, or OS. There is also a **Machine Learning Savings Plan**
      (e.g., for SageMaker).

      ### S3, Lambda & other pricing

      - **S3**: pay per **GB stored** (tiered — more volume = bigger
        discounts), per **request**, and for **data transfer out** (sending
        data *in* is free). Transfer Acceleration and lifecycle transitions
        also incur charges
      - **Lambda**: pay per **request** and for **compute duration**
        (GB-seconds — duration × memory allocated)
      - **ECS**: no fee for the service itself; pay for the underlying EC2
        instances. **Fargate**: pay per container for the CPU and memory
        allocated
      - **EBS**: pay for **provisioned** storage — volume size and IOPS are
        billed whether you use them or not

      ## The Free Tier: know the three kinds

      The exam loves Free Tier questions. There are **three different kinds**
      of free offers — do not confuse them:

      | Kind | What you get | Expires? |
      |------|--------------|----------|
      | **12 months free** | From the day you create a new account: **750 hours/month of EC2 t2.micro or t3.micro**, **5 GB of S3 Standard storage**, **750 hours/month of RDS**, **1 million Lambda requests/month** | Yes — after 12 months you pay |
      | **Always free** | e.g., **10 GB per month of CloudWatch data ingestion** (metrics/logs) and **100 GB of data transfer out** per month | No — free even after 12 months |
      | **Trials** | e.g., 30-day trials of GuardDuty, SageMaker, Inspector | Yes — short trial periods |

      The **Billing Dashboard** gives a high-level view: month-to-date cost,
      last month, forecast, and a cost breakdown per service — plus the
      **Free Tier dashboard** showing your current vs. forecasted usage
      against each free tier limit.

      :::tip **Exam tip:**
      "Free Tier" is not one uniform offer. The **12-month free tier**
      applies only to specific services with specific limits (750 hours,
      5 GB, 1 million requests...), **always-free** offers never expire, and
      **trials** last days or weeks. Also remember: some services (IAM, VPC,
      consolidated billing) are simply *free* — that is not the same as Free
      Tier.
      :::

      ## Tracking costs: tags, reports & Cost Explorer

      - **Cost Allocation Tags** track costs at a detailed level and group
        them (by team, cost center, project...). **AWS-generated tags** start
        with the prefix `aws:` (e.g., `aws:createdBy`); **user-defined tags**
        start with `user:`. A tag must be *activated* in the Billing console
        to appear in reports
      - **Cost & Usage Report (CUR)** is the **most comprehensive billing
        dataset** available: hourly or daily line items per service category,
        with metadata about pricing and reservations. It can be analyzed with
        **Athena, Redshift, or QuickSight**
      - **Cost Explorer** is the **visualization** tool: create custom
        reports of cost and usage over time — at a high level (total across
        all accounts) or at monthly, hourly, and **resource-level**
        granularity. It can **forecast usage up to 12 months** based on past
        usage and helps you choose an optimal Savings Plan
      - **Pricing Calculator** (calculator.aws) estimates the cost of a
        solution architecture — pick services, configuration, and usage and
        it returns a monthly/yearly estimate
      - **TCO Calculator** compares the **total cost of ownership** of
        running your workloads on AWS vs. on-premises (servers, storage,
        networking, IT labor) so you can justify a migration

      :::tip **Exam tip:**
      **Cost Explorer visualizes** cost/usage and forecasts it; **AWS Budgets
      alerts** you. If the question says "visualize cost trends" or "forecast
      my bill 12 months out" → Cost Explorer. If it says "notify me when I
      exceed a threshold" → Budgets. "Most comprehensive dataset, analyze
      with Athena" → Cost & Usage Report.
      :::

      ## Monitoring costs: alarms, budgets & more

      - **Billing Alarms** are simple CloudWatch alarms on the **billing
        metric**, which exists **only in `us-east-1`** but aggregates the
        cost of **all regions**. They reflect **actual cost, not projected
        cost**, and simply email you when you cross a threshold
      - **AWS Budgets** are more advanced: create **cost, usage, reservation,
        or savings plan budgets** and get alerts when the **actual or
        forecasted** amount exceeds your budget. Reservation budgets can
        track RI utilization for EC2, ElastiCache, RDS, and Redshift. Each
        budget supports **up to 5 SNS notifications** and filters by service,
        linked account, tag, region, and more. The **first two budgets are
        free**, then it costs **$0.02 per day per budget**
      - **AWS Cost Anomaly Detection** uses **machine learning** to detect
        unusual spend — one-time spikes or continuous increases — **without
        you defining thresholds**. It learns your historical patterns,
        provides root-cause analysis, and notifies via SNS (individual alerts
        or daily/weekly summaries)
      - **AWS Service Quotas** monitors your service limits; you can create
        CloudWatch alarms on quotas and **request quota increases** directly
        from the console
      - **AWS Compute Optimizer** uses ML to recommend optimal resource
        configurations (EC2 instances, Auto Scaling groups, EBS volumes,
        Lambda functions) to reduce cost and improve performance — lowering
        costs by up to **25%**, with recommendations exportable to S3
      - **AWS Marketplace** is a digital catalog of **thousands of software
        listings from independent software vendors**: custom AMIs,
        CloudFormation templates, SaaS, and containers. Purchases are added
        to **your AWS bill**, and you can sell your own solutions on it

      ## AWS Support plans

      Every account starts on **Basic Support (free)**. The exam asks you to
      pick the right plan for a scenario — memorize the prices and response
      times:

      | Plan | Price | Key features | Worst-case response |
      |------|-------|--------------|---------------------|
      | **Basic** | Free | 24/7 customer service, docs, whitepapers, forums; **7 core Trusted Advisor checks**; Personal Health Dashboard | — |
      | **Developer** | **$29/month** | Business-hours email to Cloud Support Associates; unlimited cases | General guidance < 24 business hours; system impaired < 12 business hours |
      | **Business** | **$100/month** | 24/7 phone, email, and chat; **full Trusted Advisor checks + API access**; Infrastructure Event Management (extra fee) | Production impaired < 4 hours; **production down < 1 hour** |
      | **Enterprise On-Ramp** | **$5,500/month** | Everything in Business + **pool of Technical Account Managers**, Concierge team, Well-Architected & Operations reviews | Business-critical down < **30 minutes** |
      | **Enterprise** | **$15,000/month** | Everything above + **designated Technical Account Manager**, Incident Detection & Response (extra fee) | Business-critical down < **15 minutes** |

      The 7 core Trusted Advisor checks on Basic/Developer: S3 bucket
      permissions, unrestricted security group ports, IAM use, MFA on root
      account, EBS public snapshots, RDS public snapshots, and service
      limits.

      **AWS Health** comes in two dashboards: the **Service Health
      Dashboard** (public status of all services in all regions) and the
      **Personal Health Dashboard** (events that impact *your* account and
      resources, with remediation guidance and proactive notifications).

      :::tip **Exam tip:**
      Remember the ladder: **Developer = email only (24/12 business
      hours)**; **Business = 24/7 with < 1 hour for production down**;
      **Enterprise On-Ramp = pool of TAMs and < 30 minutes**; **Enterprise =
      designated TAM and < 15 minutes**. A question about a "dedicated
      technical account manager" or "15-minute response" is always
      **Enterprise**.
      :::
