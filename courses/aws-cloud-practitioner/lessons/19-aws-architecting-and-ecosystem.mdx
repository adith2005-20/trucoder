      ## The Well-Architected Framework — guiding principles

      The **AWS Well-Architected Framework** is a set of best practices to
      help you build **secure, high-performing, resilient, and efficient**
      infrastructure for your applications. It is a *whitepaper and a
      mindset* — not a service you launch.

      **General guiding principles** (from the whitepaper — the exam likes
      these):

      - **Stop guessing your capacity needs** — use Auto Scaling to match
        actual demand instead of predicting it
      - **Test systems at production scale** — in the cloud you can spin up
        resources for an hour and test like it's real traffic
      - **Automate to make architectural experimentation easier** —
        CloudFormation (infrastructure as code) lets you recreate an
        architecture in any account or region
      - **Allow for evolutionary architectures** — design for changing
        requirements; after migrating, rethink how to leverage the cloud
        better (e.g., go serverless)
      - **Drive architectures using data** — look at actual usage patterns
        and queries, don't guess
      - **Improve through game days** — simulate stressful events (like
        flash-sale traffic) to see how your system holds up

      :::tip **Exam tip:**
      "Simulate a flash sale to stress-test the system" → **game days**
      (this is what Netflix's Chaos Monkey-style testing is about).
      "Use CloudFormation to recreate architectures easily" → **automate
      architectural experimentation**. These principle names appear
      verbatim in scenario questions.
      :::

      ## The 6 pillars — know all of them by heart

      The framework is built on **six pillars**. The exam will ask you to
      identify *which pillar* a practice belongs to, so memorize the six
      names and their one-line definitions:

      | # | Pillar | Definition (exam wording) |
      |---|--------|---------------------------|
      | 1 | **Operational Excellence** | Ability to **run and monitor systems** to deliver business value and **continuously improve** processes and procedures |
      | 2 | **Security** | Ability to **protect information, systems, and assets** while delivering business value through risk assessment and mitigation |
      | 3 | **Reliability** | Ability of a system to **recover from infrastructure or service disruptions**, dynamically acquire computing resources to meet demand, and mitigate misconfigurations or transient network issues |
      | 4 | **Performance Efficiency** | Ability to **use computing resources efficiently** to meet system requirements, maintaining efficiency as demand changes and technologies evolve |
      | 5 | **Cost Optimization** | Ability to **run systems to deliver business value at the lowest price point** |
      | 6 | **Sustainability** | Focus on **minimizing the environmental impacts** of running cloud workloads |

      The pillars are **not trade-offs to balance — they are a synergy**:
      a good architecture addresses all six together.

      :::tip **Exam tip:**
      **Name all six pillars** — operational excellence, security,
      reliability, performance efficiency, cost optimization, sustainability.
      A question listing "security + cost + reliability…" is often testing
      whether you know the *complete* set (an option with only four pillars
      is a trap). **Sustainability is the 6th pillar** — don't forget it.
      :::

      ## Pillar deep dive 1: Operational Excellence & Security

      **Operational Excellence** — run and monitor systems, continuously
      improve processes. Key design principles:

      - **Perform operations as code** — infrastructure as code
        (CloudFormation, Config) instead of manual clicks
      - **Make frequent, small, reversible changes** — so failures can be
        rolled back quickly
      - **Refine operations procedures frequently** and keep the team
        familiar with them
      - **Anticipate failure and learn from operational failures** — failing
        is learning if you take the feedback
      - Use **managed services** to reduce operational burden and implement
        **observability** (CloudWatch, X-Ray) for actionable insights
      - Key AWS services: CloudFormation, Config, CloudTrail, CloudWatch,
        X-Ray, and the Code* family (CodeCommit, CodeBuild, CodeDeploy,
        CodePipeline)

      **Security** — protect information, systems, and assets. Key design
      principles:

      - **Strong identity foundation** — centralize privilege management,
        **least privilege**, reduce reliance on long-term credentials (IAM)
      - **Enable traceability** — integrate logs and metrics so systems
        automatically respond to anomalies (CloudTrail, CloudWatch)
      - **Apply security at all layers** — edge network, VPC, subnet, load
        balancer, every instance, OS, and application
      - **Automate security best practices**
      - **Protect data in transit and at rest** — encryption, tokenization,
        access control (KMS, S3 encryption)
      - **Keep people away from data** — reduce or eliminate direct access
        and manual processing
      - **Prepare for security events** — incident response simulations and
        automation for faster detection, investigation, and recovery
      - The pillar is inseparable from the **Shared Responsibility Model**
        (AWS secures the cloud, you secure what's in the cloud)

      :::tip **Exam tip:**
      Security pillar keywords: **least privilege**, **identity
      foundation**, **data in transit and at rest**, **all layers**, and
      **shared responsibility**. If the scenario mentions MFA, IAM, or
      encryption, the pillar is **Security** — and "apply security at all
      layers" is a favorite distractor pairing with defense-in-depth.
      :::

      ## Pillar deep dive 2: Reliability & Performance Efficiency

      **Reliability** — recover from disruptions and scale to meet demand.
      Key design principles:

      - **Test recovery procedures** — use automation to simulate failures
        or recreate scenarios that led to failures
      - **Automatically recover from failure** — anticipate and remediate
        before they occur
      - **Scale horizontally** to increase aggregate availability —
        distribute requests across multiple, smaller resources so they
        don't share a single point of failure
      - **Stop guessing capacity** — maintain the optimal level to satisfy
        demand without over- or under-provisioning; use **Auto Scaling**
      - **Manage change in automation** — make infrastructure changes
        through automation so they're reliable and rollback-able
      - Key AWS services: IAM and VPC (foundations), **Service Quotas**,
        Trusted Advisor, CloudWatch, AWS Auto Scaling, CloudFormation,
        backups (S3, S3 Glacier), Route 53

      **Performance Efficiency** — use computing resources efficiently.
      Key design principles:

      - **Democratize advanced technologies** — advanced tech becomes
        managed services so you focus on product development
      - **Go global in minutes** — deploy in multiple regions quickly
        (CloudFormation)
      - **Use serverless architectures** — avoid managing servers (Lambda)
      - **Experiment more often** — easy comparative testing
      - **Mechanical sympathy** — be aware of all AWS services and choose
        the right one for each workload
      - Key AWS services: Auto Scaling, Lambda, EBS, S3, RDS (selection),
        CloudWatch (monitoring), CloudFormation (review), CloudFront,
        ElastiCache (tradeoffs)

      :::tip **Exam tip:**
      **Reliability = recover from failure + scale to meet demand**
      (Auto Scaling, testing recovery, horizontal scaling). **Performance
      Efficiency = use resources efficiently / right-size compute +
      serverless + mechanical sympathy.** Don't mix them up: a question
      about *cost* or *right-sizing instances* points to Performance
      Efficiency (efficient resource use) — while a question about
      *recovering after an outage* points to Reliability.
      :::

      ## Pillar deep dive 3: Cost Optimization & Sustainability

      **Cost Optimization** — deliver business value at the lowest price.
      Key design principles:

      - **Adopt a consumption mode** — pay only for what you use (Lambda
        bills you only when functions run, unlike a provisioned RDS
        database you pay for even when idle)
      - **Measure overall efficiency** — use CloudWatch to see if resources
        are used effectively
      - **Stop spending money on data center operations** — AWS handles
        infrastructure so you focus on applications
      - **Analyze and attribute expenditure** — **use tags** to track which
        application costs what and measure ROI
      - **Use managed and application-level services** to reduce cost of
        ownership — managed services operate at **cloud scale**, offering a
        lower cost per transaction or service
      - Key AWS services: Budgets, Cost and Usage Report, Cost Explorer,
        Spot/Reserved instances, S3 Glacier, Trusted Advisor, Auto Scaling,
        Lambda

      **Sustainability** — minimize the environmental impact of cloud
      workloads (the newest pillar). Key design principles:

      - **Understand your impact** — establish performance indicators and
        evaluate improvements (AWS Customer Carbon Footprint Tool tracks,
        measures, and forecasts your carbon emissions)
      - **Establish sustainability goals** — long-term goals per workload,
        model ROI
      - **Maximize utilization** — right-size workloads, minimize idle
        resources
      - **Adopt new, more efficient hardware and software** over time (e.g.,
        **Graviton** processors)
      - **Use managed services** — shared infrastructure is more efficient;
        automation moves infrequently-accessed data to cold storage
      - **Reduce downstream impact** — reduce energy/resources your
        customers need (e.g., no forced device upgrades)
      - Key AWS services: EC2 Auto Scaling, serverless (Lambda, Fargate),
        Cost Explorer, **Graviton**, **Spot Instances**, storage tiering
        (EFS-IA, S3 Glacier, EBS Cold HDD), **S3 Lifecycle Configurations /
        Intelligent Tiering**, Data Lifecycle Manager, and "read local,
        write global" databases (RDS Read Replicas, Aurora Global Database,
        DynamoDB Global Tables, CloudFront)

      :::tip **Exam tip:**
      Cost Optimization trigger words: **pay-as-you-go / consumption mode,
      tags, managed services at cloud scale, reserved & spot instances**.
      Sustainability trigger words: **environmental impact, carbon, energy
      efficiency, Graviton, cold storage tiers, right-size**. "Spot
      Instances use spare capacity that would otherwise be wasted" is a
      sustainability argument *and* a cost argument — both pillars accept
      it.
      :::

      ## Right Sizing & TCO vs on-premises

      **Right Sizing** — matching instance types and sizes to your workload
      performance and capacity requirements **at the lowest possible cost**:

      - EC2 has many instance types, but choosing the **most powerful** one
        is *not* the best choice — the cloud is elastic and you can change
        instance types anytime, so **start small** and scale up
      - It's also a **continuous process**: look at deployed instances and
        their metrics to find opportunities to eliminate or downsize without
        compromising capacity, quality, or performance
      - **Right-size before a cloud migration** (companies commonly migrate
        everything to the biggest instance and forget it) and **continuously
        after** (requirements change over time — review monthly)
      - Helpful tools: **CloudWatch, Cost Explorer, Trusted Advisor**, and
        third-party tools

      **Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) vs on-premises** — moving to the cloud
      reduces TCO and turns capital expense into operational expense:

      - **Trade capital expense (CAPEX) for operational expense (OPEX)** —
        pay on-demand instead of owning hardware
      - **Reduced TCO & OPEX** — no data center facilities, power, cooling,
        or hardware lifecycle to fund
      - **Economies of scale** — AWS buys infrastructure in bulk, so prices
        keep dropping and you benefit
      - The **AWS Pricing Calculator** estimates the cost of your
        architecture; the **TCO calculator** compares the total cost of
        running your workloads on AWS versus on-premises (compute, storage,
        networking, and IT labor) to build a business case for migration

      :::tip **Exam tip:**
      "Start small and scale up / match instance size to workload at lowest
      cost" → **Right Sizing**. "CAPEX → OPEX / no hardware ownership /
      economies of scale" → **TCO advantage of the cloud**. The TCO
      calculator is a *comparison tool* (AWS vs on-prem); the Pricing
      Calculator *estimates AWS costs* — don't swap them.
      :::

      ## The Well-Architected Tool & the Cloud Adoption Framework

      **AWS Well-Architected Tool** — a **free** tool to review your
      architectures against the six pillars and adopt best practices:

      - **Select a workload**, then **answer questions** about it
      - Your answers are **reviewed against the six pillars**
      - You **obtain advice**: videos, documentation, and reports, with
        results in a dashboard
      - Supports **lenses** (e.g., the Well-Architected Framework lens,
        FTR lens, Serverless lens, SaaS lens, or custom lenses)

      **AWS Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF)** — a whitepaper (not a service)
      that helps you build and execute a comprehensive plan for **digital
      transformation** through AWS, built from AWS best practices with
      thousands of customers. It has two components: **organizational
      capabilities** and **six perspectives** (you must memorize these):

      | Category | Perspective | Focus |
      |----------|-------------|-------|
      | Business capabilities | **Business** | Cloud investments accelerate digital transformation ambitions and business outcomes |
      | Business capabilities | **People** | **Bridge between technology and business** — culture, organizational structure, leadership, workforce; accelerates the cloud journey and a culture of continuous learning |
      | Business capabilities | **Governance** | Orchestrate cloud initiatives, manage risk, align with business goals |
      | Technical capabilities | **Platform** | Build and run cloud platforms (architecture, data, infrastructure) |
      | Technical capabilities | **Security** | Security, compliance, and audit |
      | Technical capabilities | **Operations** | Run and operate workloads reliably |

      :::tip **Exam tip:**
      **CAF has exactly 6 perspectives**: business, people, governance,
      platform, security, operations. The **People perspective is the
      bridge between technology and business** — a frequently asked exam
      fact. And CAF is a *whitepaper/framework*, not a service you launch:
      if the question says "which AWS *service*," CAF is a distractor.
      :::

      ## The AWS ecosystem: learning, help & partners

      **Free resources** — AWS Blogs, community Forums, Whitepapers &
      Guides, **AWS Solutions Library** (vetted technology solutions), and
      **Quick Starts** (automated, gold-standard deployments built on
      **CloudFormation** templates).

      | Resource | What it is | Exam fact |
      |----------|-----------|-----------|
      | **AWS Marketplace** | Digital catalog of **thousands of software listings from third-party vendors** (custom AMIs, CloudFormation templates, SaaS, containers) | Purchases are added to **your AWS bill**; you can also sell your own solutions |
      | **AWS Training & Certification** | Digital and classroom training, private training for organizations, **AWS Academy** for universities | Certifications validate cloud skills (e.g., Cloud Practitioner) |
      | **AWS IQ** | Find and pay **AWS Certified third-party experts** for on-demand project work | Video conferencing, contract management, secure collaboration, **integrated billing** — milestone payments go onto your AWS bill |
      | **AWS re:Post** | AWS-managed **Q&A forum** with crowd-sourced, expert-reviewed answers; **replaces the original AWS Forums** | Part of the **Free Tier**; community reputation points; questions from Premium Support customers unanswered by the community get passed to AWS engineers |
      | **AWS Knowledge Center** | Part of re:Post — the most **frequent/common questions** and requests | Best practices and troubleshooting articles |
      | **AWS Partner Network (APN)** | Partners: **Technology** (hardware, connectivity, software), **Consulting** (professional services), **Training** | AWS Professional Services is a global team of experts working alongside your team and an APN partner |
      | **AWS Managed Services (AMS)** | A **team of AWS experts** that manages and operates your infrastructure (security, monitoring, patching, backups) | Runs 24/7/365; contacted through AWS sales, not the console |

      **AWS Support plans recap** (from the billing section):

      | Plan | Key access | Response times |
      |------|-----------|----------------|
      | **Basic** | No humans — docs, forums, Trusted Advisor (limited) | — |
      | **Developer** | Business-hours email to Cloud Support Associates | General guidance < 24 business hours; system impaired < 12 business hours |
      | **Business** | **24x7 phone, email, and chat** with Cloud Support Engineers | Production system impaired < 4 hours; production system down < 1 hour |
      | **Enterprise On-Ramp** | Business plan + **pool of Technical Account Managers** | Business-critical system down < **30 minutes** |
      | **Enterprise** | Business plan + **Technical Account Manager (TAM)** + Concierge Support Team | Business-critical system down < **15 minutes** |

      :::tip **Exam tip:**
      "Find a certified expert to do project work for pay" → **AWS IQ**
      (freelancer-style, billed through AWS). "Community Q&A / forum
      answers" → **re:Post**. "Buy third-party software and pay through AWS"
      → **Marketplace**. Health-wise: the **Service Health Dashboard**
      (status.aws.amazon.com) is *public* status of AWS services, while the
      **Personal Health Dashboard** shows *events affecting your own
      account*. Support tiers: Business = 24x7 with < 1 hour for production
      down; only Enterprise gets a TAM and < 15 minutes for business-critical
      systems.
      :::
